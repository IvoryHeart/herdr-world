Cutaway

The whole street sliced open like a picture book, so every room, desk and agent is in view at once.

Town becomes one long side-on cross-section in the clean-line comic tradition: flat colour, confident outlines, hatched cut walls and mesas drifting by in parallax. Nothing is hidden. Each host is a building with a floor per room. Reception is the ground floor, where waiting agents stand with speech balloons that contain the actual question. Pneumatic tubes under the street carry those questions to the post office. A new host arrives by airship, lowered onto an empty lot. The sea at the end of the street has a ship per project, the lighthouse sweeps the town at night, and fish swim past the release cables. This is the editorial-illustration revival of the 2020s, and it is the most legible option: on a phone the cutaway becomes a strip you scroll sideways, like reading a comic.

The whole street

From left: pine arriving by airship, the apartments, the lounge, ATLAS (reception plus world, crew, lab and docs), the post office, the town hall (hiring and expenses), NIMBUS, the library with its memory archive below, the PR warehouse, then the quay, the sea and a ship per project. The question tubes run under the street. On a phone the view drifts along the street.

The cast

Profile figures in long coats. The harness shows in the coat colour and the hat: a fedora for Claude, a ball cap for Codex, a twin-bobble beanie for Gemini, a spiky hood for Rex. States are comic grammar everyone already reads: a thought cloud, a speech balloon, Z's, a stamped page.

Close-ups

Lit rooms have wallpaper and a skirting board in the project colour, a lamp and glowing screens. A dark room is navy and says “empty”. The section walls are hatched like an architect's drawing.

Night, in the rain

Ink-line rain, puddle rings and umbrellas in harness colours. The street lamps pool light, the bus headlights cut across, and the lighthouse sweeps the whole street.
