Pop-Up

The town is a pop-up book open on your desk. Everything in it is cut, folded and glued by hand.

Buildings are folded card. Rooms are open-top shoebox dioramas, so you look straight down into every desk. Agents are sticker cut-outs on fold stands. Questions fly to the post office as paper planes. The sea is a set of theatre wave strips sliding past each other, and a new host pops up off the page when you pull its tab. At night a reading lamp falls across the page and light glows through the paper. In 2026 everything on a screen is glossy and generated, so a world that looks handmade, with grain, shadows and slightly wonky cuts, reads as warm and trustworthy. It also suits a tool that watches over your crew.

The whole town

Two hosts (ATLAS and NIMBUS), each a folded facade over its rooms. The town hall stands on a doily plaza, the lounge sits bottom left, then the post office and apartments, the library, and the PR warehouse on the quay. The sea on the right page has one lane per project. A third host, pine, is popping up bottom left.

The cast

One silhouette and four harnesses, told apart by colour and hat: a beret for Claude, a mortarboard for Codex, star antennae for Gemini, a crest for Rex. Each state has its own paper prop: a thought cloud on a wire, a yellow “?” pennant, a green check disc, a mug, an umbrella, and “z”s on threads.

Close-ups

A host's rooms are trays, two at ground level and two raised on a plinth behind them. A lit room has wallpaper in its project's colour, a paper lamp and glowing laptop screens. An empty room goes grey-blue. Reception is the counter in front, with its queue oldest first.

Night, in the rain

The page goes blue and the reading lamp comes on. Busy rooms glow through their paper and the boats show their mast lights. The rain falls as threads with paper droplets, and walkers carry umbrellas in their harness colour.
